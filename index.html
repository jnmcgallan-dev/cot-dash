How to read this dashboard

What this is. The weekly US Commitment of Traders (COT) report from the CFTC (released Fridays, ~3:30pm ET) for 41 markets across 6 sectors, using the futures and options combined report so options-inclusive positioning is included. It shows how speculators (momentum / trend traders) and commercials (producers / hedgers) are positioned, measured against each market’s own trailing 3-year range.

New to this? Start here

  • Speculators — traders betting on price direction (hedge funds, trend-followers, retail). They don’t use the underlying commodity or currency — they’re purely trying to profit from the move.
  • Commercials — businesses with real exposure to the asset (an airline hedging fuel costs, an exporter hedging currency risk). They trade to reduce risk, not to speculate.
  • Open interest — the total number of futures contracts currently open. It’s the “size of the pool” that positions are measured against.
  • Net position — long contracts minus short contracts for a group. Positive = net long, negative = net short.
  • Percentile — where today’s net position ranks against the same market’s last 3 years of weekly readings. 90th percentile means more crowded (long or short) than 90% of the last 3 years.

Key numbers

  • Percentile (e.g. “92nd”) — where current spec positioning sits within that market’s own 3-year history. 50th = typical; higher = more long than usual; lower = more short than usual.
  • Extreme Long = percentile ≥ 90. Extreme Short = percentile ≤ 10.
  • The bar shows the spec percentile, radiating from the 50th-percentile center line. The thin tick is the commercial percentile.

Signals to look for

  • Amber / “Ext Long” — speculators crowded long.
  • Brick / “Ext Short” — speculators crowded short.
  • Teal / “Confluence” — specs and commercials are at opposite extremes. This is the classic contrarian watch: the crowd is crowded on one side while hedgers lean the other way. Worth investigating.
  • ▲ / ▼ + pp — how much spec net positioning moved this week (in percentage points of open interest).

How to use it

  • Click any market to expand its 3-year sparkline (spec vs commercial) and full stat breakdown.
  • Press Refresh live data to pull the latest CFTC report. Until a refresh succeeds, sample data (labeled) is shown.
  • The pill at the top tells you whether you are viewing live or sample data, and the report date.

Caveats

  • Descriptive, not a signal. An extreme can persist through a long trend; this dashboard flags conditions to investigate, not trades to take.
  • Legacy-report “commercial” data in commodities blends true hedgers with swap dealers.
  • Percentiles require at least 52 weeks of history. A market showing no data usually means its registry code needs updating against the current CFTC report.
Descriptive only — not a trading signal.
